A compartment can be sealed by more than one O-ring (redundant seals, separate ports, different compounds) — add one card per O-ring; their permeation losses add together. Squeezing deforms the round cord into an equal-area ellipse, and the gas crosses along its major axis, so the diffusion path is d2 ÷ (1 − squeeze): a thicker cord or more squeeze both lengthen it (less permeation). A taller exposed face (w) means more area, increasing permeation — see the drawings below.

What these O-ring dimensions mean (drawings)

Gas crosses the squeezed cord along the ellipse's long axis — that is the permeation path L. The exposed face height w is the height of the rubber face it enters through, measured along the squeeze axis, at right angles to the flow.

In "auto" mode w is half the ellipse's perimeter — the arc facing the high-pressure side. That arc scales with d2 exactly as L does, so auto mode is independent of cord diameter; use manual when comparing cords in a fixed gland.

Estimation tool for engineering screening purposes — not a substitute for manufacturer permeation test data or a qualified seal design review. See README.md for the full derivation, assumptions and unit conventions. All calculations run locally in your browser; no data is sent anywhere.
